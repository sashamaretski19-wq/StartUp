The problem

You have the numbers. You don't have the answer.

The model is opaque

Dashboards are built, cohorts are exported, inference invoices keep arriving. But asked whether your current CAC pays back at your current retention, you say “probably” — and you don't believe yourself.

The gap keeps widening

Acquisition is getting expensive faster than retention is improving. This isn't one bad month, it's a trend — and every month you don't know the exact numbers costs more than the one before.

Why it landed now: there are many times more consumer AI products, they compete for the same placements, and the platforms keep raising their rates — CAC is up across every channel. That used to be covered by round money. It works worse now. And on top of it sits a cost line classic SaaS never had at all.

How it works

Four steps and one document at the end

  1. I take what you already have Cohorts, spend broken down by channel, inference invoices, product analytics. No new trackers to install — I work with your data as it is.
  2. I build the model CAC by channel → retention by cohort → LTV net of variable COGS per user. Every assumption on its own line, not buried inside a formula.
  3. I find the break-even condition At what retention, ticket size and inference cost the model turns positive — and how far you are from that point today.
  4. I hand over the deck and the roadmap What to fix first, what second, and what not to touch at all. Prioritised for two quarters, not a list of forty recommendations.
